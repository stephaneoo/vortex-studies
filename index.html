Film inspirations

2001: A Space Odyssey · Stanley Kubrick, 1968

The Star Gate sequence, made by Douglas Trumbull with a slit-scan machine: a camera creeping toward a narrow slit while backlit artwork slid behind it, one long exposure per frame, stretching flat art into an endless corridor of light. → Slit-Scan Gate

Contact · Robert Zemeckis, 1997

Ellie Arroway's ride through the Machine: a chain of translucent, rippling wormholes that twist and branch before opening onto another star system. → Wormhole Drive

Interstellar · Christopher Nolan, 2014

Gargantua, the black hole rendered from physicist Kip Thorne's equations, with its accretion disk bent up and over by gravity. It shaped the lensing at the end of each wormhole. → Wormhole Drive

Sketched along the way

Star Wars · George Lucas, 1977

The jump to hyperspace: stars stretching into streaks as the ship punches through. The archetypal speed tunnel.

Arrival · Denis Villeneuve, 2016

The heptapods' circular logograms, exhaled like ink in fog. Tried as a vortex made of the ink itself.

Enter the Void · Gaspar Noé, 2009

A disembodied drift through neon Tokyo, falling into lights and out the other side. Tried for its palette, from inside the light.

The Tree of Life · Terrence Malick, 2011

The birth-of-the-universe sequence, with practical effects overseen by Douglas Trumbull: dyes, fluids and chemicals shot up close instead of rendered.

Still to try

Vertigo · Alfred Hitchcock, 1958

Saul Bass's title spirals, drawn with John Whitney's mechanical analog computer. Hypnotic geometry before any digital tools.

Doctor Who titles · BBC, 1963

Bernard Lodge's howlaround: a TV camera pointed at its own monitor, feeding back into a churning tunnel of light.

Altered States · Ken Russell, 1980

Sensory-deprivation trips that dissolve the body into fire, spirals and primordial matter.

Brainstorm · Douglas Trumbull, 1983

Trumbull's own film: recorded memories played back as wide, rushing first-person streams.

Koyaanisqatsi · Godfrey Reggio, 1982

Time-lapse cities turned into rivers of light. A vortex made of real footage.

The Fountain · Darren Aronofsky, 2006

The nebulas are macro photographs of chemical reactions in petri dishes by Peter Parks, not CGI. Close to the "ultraterrestrial" idea.

Doctor Strange · Scott Derrickson, 2016

The fall through the multiverse: kaleidoscopic, fractal, folding space.

Annihilation · Alex Garland, 2018

The lighthouse finale: a slowly mutating, refracting presence that mirrors whoever faces it.

Under the Skin · Jonathan Glazer, 2013

The black liquid void: a slow sink into dark, glossy nothing, the opposite of a light tunnel.
